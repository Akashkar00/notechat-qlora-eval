# About the dataset

This explains what's in the dataset in plain language. For exact numbers,
see `docs/data_report.md` (auto-generated — don't hand-edit it). For the
raw source file and format, see `src/data/build_dataset.py`.

## What the model is learning to do

Given a **clinical note** (a doctor's written summary of a patient visit),
generate a realistic **doctor-patient conversation** that matches it — the
kind of back-and-forth dialogue that could have produced that note.

So each training example is a pair:

- **Input:** a clinical note (~1,700 characters on average)
- **Output:** a conversation between "Doctor:" and "Patient:" (~2,300
  characters on average, about 20 turns)

## Where it comes from

The data comes from **NoteChat**, a public research dataset. Importantly,
the conversations were **not recorded from real patient visits** — they
were generated by an LLM pipeline that worked backwards from real clinical
notes to synthesize a plausible conversation that could have led to that
note. The notes are real; the dialogues are AI-generated.

This matters for how the model's output should be judged later: since the
"correct answer" dialogue is itself just one AI's guess at what the
conversation might have looked like, it's a *reasonable example* to learn
from, not ground truth in the way a transcript of an actual visit would
be. Scoring the fine-tuned model by how closely it matches these
dialogues tells you how similar it is to that one synthetic example, not
how medically accurate or realistic it is.

## How big it is

- 10,000 clinical notes to start, all of them kept (no duplicates found)
- Split into:
  - **8,000** for training
  - **1,000** for validation (checking progress during training)
  - **1,000** for testing (final evaluation, untouched during training)
- The split is done by note, and a fixed random seed is used, so the same
  notes always land in the same split every time the data is rebuilt —
  nothing leaks between train/val/test.

## Example

Here's one real training pair (`note-0210a7fec4b37ec0` in `train.parquet`),
shortened only where marked with `[...]`:

**Clinical note (input):**

> A 73-year-old female patient complained of a painful, non-healing growth
> at her left side inner cheek region for three months. The patient had a
> history of usage of betel nut since childhood. [...] Intraoral
> examination revealed a sharp tooth-26, and a 4 x 4 cm tender and
> indurated ulcero-proliferative growth at left mid buccal mucosa region
> [...] Biopsy confirmed well-differentiated SCC. TNM staging: IVA- T4a
> N1 M0.

**Conversation (output):**

> Doctor: Hello, how are you feeling today?
> Patient: I'm not feeling too good, doctor. I have a painful growth on
> my left cheek that hasn't healed for three months.
> Doctor: I see. Can you tell me more about your history with betel nut?
> Patient: I've been using it since childhood.
> [...]
> Doctor: During your examination, we found a tender and indurated
> ulcero-proliferative growth on your left mid buccal mucosa region.
> [...] Unfortunately, the biopsy confirmed a diagnosis of
> well-differentiated SCC, which is a malignant non-healing
> ulcero-proliferative growth.
> Patient: Oh no, what does that mean?
> Doctor: It means that we need to start treatment immediately. [...]

Notice the conversation basically restates the note's facts as things the
doctor says or asks — that's the pattern (and the limitation) across the
whole dataset, since the AI that generated it was working backwards from
the note rather than imagining an independent visit.

## A couple of quirks that were cleaned up

- A tiny number of conversations (18 out of 10,000) had a stray line of
  leftover text at the very start, before the dialogue actually began —
  a small leak from however the AI that generated NoteChat was prompted.
  That stray line is automatically stripped before training.
- Every note is checked for exact duplicates so the same note can't
  accidentally end up in more than one split.
