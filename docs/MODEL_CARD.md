## Intended use

Generating a plausible doctor-patient **dialogue** from a clinical note, in
NoteChat's house style, **as a research artifact for evaluating fine-tuning
methodology**. The deliverable of this project is the eval harness; this
adapter is the evidence that the harness works (`PROJECT_SPEC.md` §0).

Reasonable uses:

- Benchmarking small-model adaptation against larger zero-shot models.
- Generating synthetic dialogue for **downstream research** where the
  synthetic, unverified nature of the output is understood and preserved.
- A worked example of QLoRA on a consumer GPU.

## Out-of-scope use — read this before anything else

**This model must not be used in any clinical, diagnostic, documentation, or
patient-facing setting.** It is not a medical device, has had no clinical
validation, and no clinician reviewed its outputs.

Specifically it must not be used to:

- Produce or draft any part of a real patient record.
- Simulate a real consultation, or generate patient-facing advice.
- Stand in for a clinician, scribe, or medical transcription system.
- Generate training data for any system that touches patient care.

**It fabricates clinical values.** The Phase 5 sanity check caught it
appending an invented aside with no basis in the source note, and the
faithfulness metrics in `artifacts/eval/` quantify how much of the note's
numeric content survives into the dialogue and how much is made up. Fluent,
correctly-formatted output that states a wrong lab value is the *expected*
failure mode here, not an edge case.

## Training data

- **Source:** NoteChat, `our_revised_v2.csv` (10,000 note/dialogue pairs).
  Clinical notes originate from PMC-Patients case reports (public, PubMed
  Central).
- **Splits:** 8,000 / 1,000 / 1,000 train/val/test, split by `note_id`,
  seed 42, zero overlap asserted in `tests/test_data.py`.
- **Cleaning:** exact-duplicate notes dropped by content hash (0 found); 18
  dialogues (0.18%) had a generation-artifact preamble stripped.
- **Full statistics:** `docs/data_report.md` (auto-generated).
- No raw training content is reproduced in this repo (`PROJECT_SPEC.md`
  §1.1); `data/` is gitignored and enforced by a pre-commit hook.

### The central data caveat

**The training targets are themselves LLM-generated.** NoteChat's dialogues
were synthesized backwards from real notes by a multi-agent LLM pipeline —
they are not transcripts and were not verified by clinicians. This model
therefore imitates *another model's output*, and every reference-based
metric (ROUGE, BERTScore) measures **similarity to one synthetic exemplar**,
not correctness. See `PROJECT_SPEC.md` §4.2 and `docs/about_dataset.md`.

A consequence worth stating plainly: the fine-tuned model beating a larger
zero-shot model on those metrics substantially reflects having learned this
corpus's house style, which the larger model has never seen. That is a real
and useful finding about adaptation efficiency; it is **not** evidence of
better clinical dialogue generation.

## Evaluation

Full results and method: `README.md` and `artifacts/eval/comparison.md`.

Four arms, all scored on the same 200 held-out records with greedy
(deterministic) decoding:

1. Zero-shot Qwen2.5-3B — floor
2. Zero-shot Qwen2.5-14B — ~4.7× parameter scale comparison
3. **This adapter** — QLoRA fine-tuned Qwen2.5-3B
4. TF-IDF nearest-neighbour retrieval — no model at all

Metrics: ROUGE-1/2/L and BERTScore (vs. the reference dialogue),
turn-format validity (structural), and numeric faithfulness (vs. the
clinical note — the only real artifact in the dataset). All aggregates carry
1,000-resample 95% bootstrap CIs, and all cross-arm claims are paired
bootstrap deltas.

**Arm 4 is the result to pay attention to.** A retrieval baseline with no
model scores competitively on ROUGE while carrying over almost none of the
query note's numbers — because a retrieved dialogue is fluent, well-formed,
on-topic, and about a *different patient*. Any reading of this card that
treats a high ROUGE score as evidence of quality is refuted by that arm.

## Contamination

NoteChat is public and its notes come from PMC-Patients, so the base model
may have encountered the corpus in pretraining. Probed in
`docs/contamination_report.md` (`python -m src.eval.contamination`) via
prefix-continuation against a random-continuation control. See that report
for the finding and its limitations.

## Known limitations

- Fabricates clinical values (quantified; see above).
- Learned one corpus's house style; will not generalise to another
  dialogue format without re-tuning.
- Evaluated on 200 records (justified in `configs/eval.yaml`), English only,
  case-report-derived notes only — not real-world clinic notes, which are
  messier, abbreviated, and structured differently.
- The faithfulness metric checks *numbers*; a fabricated diagnosis stated
  without a number is not caught.
- No human evaluation was performed. Phase 4 was skipped deliberately —
  a human rating against a synthetic reference would not make the reference
  authoritative (`PROJECT_SPEC.md` §7a item 6).
- No LoRA rank / epoch ablations were run, so these hyperparameters are a
  reasonable starting configuration, not a tuned optimum.
